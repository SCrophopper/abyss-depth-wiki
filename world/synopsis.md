# Synopsis

Long ago, in an unspecified century, humanity faced an unprecedented cataclysmic event. Earth's sea surface rose beyond its limit, and the troposphere began to expand along with other atmospheric layers. What was once a world of lush landscapes and sprawling cities stood on the brink of a watery abyss.

As civilization evolved, it became a melting pot of cultures and backgrounds. Former surface inhabitants joined pioneering scientists and engineers on a journey into the depths, harnessing the geothermal energy radiating from Earth's core.

The result was a subterranean society that defied conventional boundaries. Immense underground cities, bathed in the soft glow of bioluminescent technology, arose within colossal caverns. Artificial day-night cycles and subterranean gardens provided a semblance of the old surface world while the population continued to grow and diversify.

Advanced materials and technology enabled pressure-resistant habitats, extensive submerged transportation networks, and sanctuaries for conserved surface animals. Yet the scarcity of food, oxygen, fresh water, and habitable space became a stark reality—and access to those essentials became a source of tension.

At the heart of the conflict is the disparity between people living in advanced, self-sustaining habitats and those in makeshift communities atop partially submerged structures. The former enjoy relative comfort and access to advanced technology; the latter struggle for basic necessities.

Resentment grows among marginalized communities. Protests and civil disobedience erupt as they demand fairer resource distribution and political power. A charismatic leader emerges from the disenfranchised and rallies support. The conflict threatens both the fragile remnants of humanity and their collective chance of survival.

Others choose a different life on the surface. Commonly called [Refugees]({{ '/world/refugees/' | relative_url }}), they hold to a more conventional existence on connected floating rafts. Their communities include hydroponic farms, fisheries, schools, recycling facilities, and livestock farms.
